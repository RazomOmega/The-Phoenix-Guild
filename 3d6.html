Every roll on the bell curve

Three six-sided dice don't roll flat. The chance of hitting a target number swings from near-certain to hopeless across just a few points, and the same +1 that barely matters at the edges is worth a full eighth of the odds in the middle. Here is the whole surface at once.

How to read it

A cell answers one question: with this bonus, how often do you meet or beat this target? Meets it beats it — a total exactly equal to the target counts as a hit. If your system makes players exceed the target instead, just read one column to the right.

Everything on the table depends only on TN − bonus, the number you need on the raw dice. That's why the bands run in clean diagonals, and why the outlined diagonal — where the dice need an 11 — is exactly even odds every time.

The steep middle is the design lever. Between a 25% shot and a 75% shot lie only about four points of target number, so in that band a single point of bonus or a −2 penalty is felt immediately. Out past TN − bonus = 16 or below 6, whole points of modifier barely move the needle — which is where you want situational bonuses that shouldn't dominate.

Set routine tasks around 6–8 above the expected bonus, hard ones around 11, and reserve 15+ for feats that should mostly fail. A character with +0 hits a TN 15 once in twelve tries.

Exact odds — every value is a count out of 216 outcomes, not a simulation. Assumes no criticals, no exploding dice, no rerolls.
